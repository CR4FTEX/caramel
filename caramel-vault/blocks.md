# Blocks

How Caramel adds a block and its item, gives it a look, drops, tags and a name, and renames vanilla's blocks, and the traps each part hides. The code is `io.github.cr4ftex.caramel.block.StoneFamily`, with the datagen providers `CaramelBlockFamilies`, `CaramelModelProvider`, `CaramelBlockLootProvider`, `CaramelBlockTagProvider`, `CaramelItemTagProvider`, `CaramelLanguageProvider` and `CaramelVanillaNamesProvider`; the decisions behind them are in idea C-010 in `docs/DESIGN.md`. Read in the decompiled 26.3 classes and checked in the game tests `StoneFamilyGameTest` and `VanillaOverridesGameTest` (see [[gametest]]).

## Registering a block

- A block is `Registry.register(BuiltInRegistries.BLOCK, key, factory.apply(properties.setId(key)))`, as vanilla's public `Blocks.register(ResourceKey, Function, Properties)` does. The id fixes the block's loot table, `<namespace>:blocks/<path>`, and its name key, `block.<namespace>.<path>`, unless the properties override them.
- Its item is a `BlockItem` with `new Item.Properties().setId(itemKey).useBlockDescriptionPrefix()`, so it shows the block's name. `block.asItem()` reads `Item.BY_BLOCK`; vanilla's private `Items.registerItem` fills it with `blockItem.registerBlocks(Item.BY_BLOCK, item)`, and for every other mod's block item Fabric's registry sync does the same as the item is registered (`BlockItemTracker` in `fabric-registry-sync-v0`). With `StoneFamily` making that call itself as well, and without it, every game test passed (C-010's probe).
- The registries stay writable while mods initialize ([[tools]], "Registering an item"), so `StoneFamily` creates its blocks in static fields and `Caramel.onInitialize` loads the class by calling `StoneFamily.register()`.
- Vanilla builds each piece of a family with a private helper of `Blocks`: stairs with `registerLegacyStair`, `Properties.ofLegacyCopy(base)` plus `isViewBlocking(NEAR_PLANE_INTERSECTS_OUTLINE)`; slabs with `registerSlab`, the same, some with a harder `destroyTime` (the stone and stone brick slabs take 2.0 against their blocks' 1.5); walls with `registerWall`, `ofLegacyCopy(base).forceSolidOn()`; the stone button with `ButtonBlock(BlockSetType.STONE, 20, Blocks.buttonProperties())`; the stone pressure plate with `PressurePlateBlock(BlockSetType.STONE, ...)` and properties of its own; and the nether brick fence with its block's values written out. `StoneFamily` repeats each, and copies the private `NEAR_PLANE_INTERSECTS_OUTLINE`, a test of whether the block's outline meets the camera's near plane.
- `Properties.ofLegacyCopy(block)` copies the physical properties: hardness, blast resistance, map colour, sound, collision, push reaction, whether the right tool is needed, instrument and the rest. `Properties.ofFullCopy(block)` adds the behaviour tests (`isViewBlocking`, `isSuffocating`, `isRedstoneConductor`, `isValidSpawn`, post-processing, jump factor) and also the `drops` and `descriptionId` fields (`BlockBehaviour.Properties`).
- Fabric's `CreativeModeTabEvents.modifyOutputEvent(tab)` places items with `output.insertAfter(existing, items...)`, in the order given.

## Families for datagen

- `net.minecraft.data.BlockFamily.Builder` is public: `new BlockFamily.Builder(base).stairs(...).slab(...).wall(...).getFamily()` describes a family for the model and recipe providers without adding it to `BlockFamilies.getAllFamilies()`, the list C-001 to C-009 walk and count ([[recipes]]). `CaramelBlockFamilies` holds C-010's: each base block with Caramel's pieces only.

## Models

- `BlockModelGenerators.family(block)` starts a `BlockFamilyProvider` by writing the full block's own blockstate and model (`fullBlock`), then `generateFor(family)` writes each variant's. For a vanilla base block that writes a `minecraft:` blockstate and model into Caramel's pack, over vanilla's; see "Traps". `CaramelModelProvider` builds `blockModels.new BlockFamilyProvider(TextureMapping.cube(base))` instead, which writes only the variants, with the vanilla texture (`minecraft:block/stone` for the stone wall). The slab variant needs the full block's model for the double slab and throws "Full block not generated yet" without it; C-010 adds no slab to a vanilla block.
- `generateFor` skips a variant that is another vanilla family's base block, and writes nothing for a variant with no model shape, such as a family's door or sign when the family has none (`SHAPE_CONSUMERS`).
- `createRotatedPillarWithHorizontalVariant(block, TexturedModel.COLUMN, TexturedModel.COLUMN_HORIZONTAL)` writes a pillar as a log is written: textures `<id>_side` and `<id>_top`, and a model of its own for lying on its side (`TextureMapping.column`).
- A block's item takes the block's model unless one is written for it: buttons, walls and fences write an inventory model, and stairs, slabs and pressure plates reuse a block model.

## A vanilla block's look

- A vanilla block's blockstate names its models by id, and a model names its textures. Caramel changes a vanilla block's look by shipping its own copy of the models at their vanilla ids, which replace vanilla's as a resource pack's do, and leaves vanilla's blockstate and texture files alone: Smooth Stone's four models (`smooth_stone`, `smooth_stone_slab`, `smooth_stone_slab_top`, `smooth_stone_slab_double`) name `caramel:block/smooth_stone` on every face, with vanilla's parents (`CaramelModelProvider`; vanilla's own models read from `minecraft-clientOnly`'s `assets/minecraft/`).
- Vanilla's texture file is then free for another block to name: Polished Stone's models are vanilla's four smooth stone models under Caramel's ids, so it wears the old look, the slab's `smooth_stone_slab_side` included, without a copy of Mojang's pixels in Caramel ([[licensing]]). Vanilla builds that slab with its own helper, `BlockModelGenerators.createSmoothStoneSlab`, which Caramel repeats.
- A resource pack that retextures `minecraft:block/smooth_stone` now changes Polished Stone, and one that replaces Smooth Stone's models wins over Caramel's, whichever sits higher.
- A test server's `minecraft` mod container holds no assets; the client jar is on the game test classpath, and its file is the class loader's resource whose URL contains `minecraft-clientOnly`, beside Caramel's copy at the same path (`StoneFamilyGameTest.vanillaModel`).

## Drops

- `CaramelBlockLootProvider` extends Fabric's `FabricBlockLootSubProvider`. A table added for any block is written at that block's loot table id, so `add(Blocks.STONE_STAIRS, ...)` writes `data/minecraft/loot_table/blocks/stone_stairs.json`, which replaces vanilla's: for each id only the top pack's file is read ([[recipes]], "How recipes load"). Only Caramel's own blocks are checked for a missing table (`FabricBlockLootSubProvider.generate`).
- Vanilla's block loot sets a random sequence named after each table, `"random_sequence": "minecraft:blocks/<id>"`, and Fabric's provider writes none; `CaramelBlockLootProvider.add` sets it on every table.
- `createSingleItemTableWithSilkTouch(block, drop)` gives the block itself with Silk Touch and the other item otherwise, as vanilla's stone does with cobblestone. `createSlabItemTable(slab)` gives 2 for a double slab. No vanilla helper does both: the Silk Touch branch of `createSelfDropDispatchTable` is a plain item, so a double slab would give 1. `slabWithSilkTouch` puts the double-slab count on both branches.

## Tags

- `CaramelBlockTagProvider` (a `FabricTagsProvider.BlockTagsProvider`) adds to vanilla's block tags; as for items ([[tools]]), a tag file adds to a tag and replaces nothing. `CaramelItemTagProvider` takes the block tag provider and copies tags with `copy(BlockItemTagId)`. `BlockItemTags` holds the paired ids, `STAIRS`, `SLABS`, `WALLS`, `FENCES`, `STONE_BUTTONS` and more, which `ItemTags` lacks ([[gametest]], "Traps").
- Vanilla lists its full stone blocks one by one in `#minecraft:blocks_motion_no_leaves` (stone, smooth_stone, `#stone_bricks`, quartz_pillar, the deepslate tiles and more), and takes its stairs, slabs, walls, fences and pressure plates in by their tags. On it hang `blocks_motion`, the heightmap tags `blocks_motion_in_heightmap` and its `_no_leaves`, `causes_suffocation`, `blocks_fluid_flow`, `blocks_lava_fire_spread`, `blocks_dolphin_jump`, `entities_can_teleport_to` and `ice_melts_when_destroyed_above`. In 26.3 the heightmaps read that tag (`Heightmap.Types`), and rain, snow layers and lightning read the heightmap (`Level.precipitationAt`, `ServerLevel.tickPrecipitation`, `findLightningTargetAround`), so a full block outside it lets rain and snow through. Its item goes in `#minecraft:sulfur_cube_archetype/slow_bouncy`, as the stone bricks', for a sulfur cube to swallow it (`SulfurCube.isSwallowableItem`).
- `#minecraft:buttons` holds `#minecraft:stone_buttons`, and `#minecraft:pressure_plates` holds `#minecraft:stone_pressure_plates`, so a stone button or plate joins the smaller tag. `#minecraft:fences` holds the wooden fences' tag and the nether brick fence, and two fences join up when both or neither are in `#minecraft:wooden_fences` (`FenceBlock`), so a stone fence there joins the nether brick fence and not a wooden one.

## Names

- New blocks and items get their names from `CaramelLanguageProvider`, in `assets/caramel/lang/en_us.json`, under the keys their ids give.
- A vanilla block keeps its id and gets a new name under its own key, such as `block.minecraft.cobblestone`. The client merges translations for each namespace in turn, and in each namespace every pack's file from vanilla up, later keys winning (`ClientLanguage.loadFrom`, `appendFrom`). A key in `assets/minecraft/lang/en_us.json` in Caramel's pack therefore wins over vanilla's own, since mod resources sit above vanilla's; in `assets/caramel/lang/`, it would win only if the client lists the `caramel` namespace after `minecraft`. `CaramelVanillaNamesProvider` writes the first, as a `FabricCodecDataProvider` with `PackOutput.Target.RESOURCE_PACK`, the folder `lang` and an unbounded string map.
- The server reads vanilla's `en_us.json`, then every `assets/<namespace>/lang/en_us.json` of each mod over it (Fabric's server `LanguageMixin`, `ServerLanguageUtil.getModLanguageFiles`), so a test's `Language.getInstance()` sees the new names too.
- A rename changes English only; other languages keep vanilla's names. The creative and recipe book searches match an item's name, and its id only when the text holds a colon (`IdSearchTree.search`, through `SessionSearchTrees`): after a rename, "cobblestone" finds nothing, and "minecraft:cobblestone" or ":cobblestone" finds Cobbled Stone (C-010's playtest).
- Renaming an id is not done: a world saved with a renamed id loses those blocks to air without Caramel, and Fabric API's `FabricRegistry.addAlias(old, new)` only makes an extra id resolve when looked up (`get`, `getValue`, `containsKey`): saves, F3 and tab-completion keep the real id (`MappedRegistryMixin` in `fabric-registry-sync-v0`, `ItemParser`, `BlockStateParser`).

## Traps

- **`family(vanillaBlock)` silently replaces the vanilla block's blockstate and models.** (Smooth Stone's models are replaced on purpose, by plain model templates; see "A vanilla block's look".) Datagen raises nothing. With `blockModels.family(Blocks.STONE).generateFor(CaramelBlockFamilies.STONE)` for the stone wall, it wrote `assets/minecraft/blockstates/stone.json`, `models/block/stone.json` and `models/block/stone_mirrored.json`, the last from stone's custom generator in `FULL_BLOCK_MODEL_CUSTOM_GENERATORS`. `StoneFamilyGameTest.everyNewBlockAndItemShipsItsAssetDefinitions` fails on it: "Caramel ships [blockstates/stone.json, lang/en_us.json, models/block/stone.json, models/block/stone_mirrored.json] under assets/minecraft, expected only lang/en_us.json" (deliberate break). Start from the texture for a vanilla block's pieces.
- **A Caramel block left out of the loot provider drops nothing, silently.** `FabricBlockLootSubProvider` checks for missing tables only with strict validation, which `build.gradle` does not turn on: with cracked stone tiles left out, datagen deleted its table and logged only `removed stale`, and the block broke into nothing. `StoneFamilyGameTest.blocksDropAsTheOwnerSays` fails: "caramel:cracked_stone_tiles with an iron pickaxe drops [], expected 1 caramel:cracked_stone_tiles", and the same with Silk Touch (deliberate break).
- **`Properties.ofFullCopy` carries over another block's loot table and name** where that block overrides them (`drops`, `descriptionId`), as vanilla's wall torches and other `wallVariant` blocks do. The copy then drops and is named as that block, silently (read in `BlockBehaviour.Properties`; not reproduced). `StoneFamily` copies with `ofLegacyCopy` and adds the view-blocking rule itself.
- **A new full block left out of `#minecraft:blocks_motion_no_leaves` lets rain and snow through, silently.** It is solid to walk on, but the heightmaps skip it, so snow settles under a roof of it, a fire under it goes out in rain and lightning strikes below it (read in `Heightmap`, `Level` and `ServerLevel`, found in review; not seen in game). `StoneFamilyGameTest.newBlocksJoinTheTagsOfTheirKind` compares each full block's tags with the vanilla block it is made like: with the tag removed it failed 48 of 262 checks, "caramel:honed_stone is not in block tag #minecraft:blocks_dolphin_jump, which minecraft:smooth_stone is in" and the rest (deliberate break).
